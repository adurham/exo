## 4. Why 300K collapses for 1017

Same row, both `cached=98,304`:

| | Sept | 1017 |
|---|---|---|
| `peak_memory_usage` per rank | 123.7-129.5 GB | 135.5-145.5 GB |
| decode round (`EXO_PROFILER` window) | 194-206 ms | 1,274-1,380 ms |
| page-ins during the decode window | — | 59,086 (~970 MB) |

The box has 137 GB and `iogpu.wired_limit_mb=120000` (125.8 GB). Sept runs fine at
~124-129 GB, so the ceiling is the box's RAM, not the sysctl.

**The KV cache is not the reason.** At 300K the whole `SessionCache` is 1.8 GB —
`comp_kv` 0.61 GB (layer 20) + 0.31 GB on each of layers 2/8/14, `index_k` 0.15 /
3×0.08 GB, window rings ~0. A deep walk of everything reachable from the cache
(`[MEMPROF deep]`) finds nothing else above 50 MB.

**The unattributed part is ~19 GB.** After load the process reports active 111.2 GB
(1017) / 108.9 GB (Sept). At 300K turn end: 132.3 GB (1017) / 123.0 GB (Sept). Cache
accounts for 1.8 GB of that, so ~19 GB (1017) and ~14 GB (Sept) are live arrays
outside the session cache. 1017 starts 2.3 GB higher and grows ~8 GB more than Sept
at the same context, which is what pushes it over the ceiling.

`ModelCache.ensure_capacity` grows geometrically to `max(req, 2*capacity)`, so a 300K
session allocates 524,288 rows rather than 300,003 (+0.95 GB) — worth fixing, but two
orders below the gap.

## 5. The two defects, separated

### 5a. Prefill retained ~20 GB of DSpark taps (fixed, opt-in)

`session.prefill()` collected every chunk's tap dicts into a `taps` list
(`taps_out=taps`) for the whole turn. Nothing reads that list back — the per-chunk
callback already feeds the draft window — and it is O(context) hidden states.
`EXO_DSV41_PREFILL_TAPS_LIST=0` keeps only the last chunk's taps (the object stays
non-None so `want_taps` remains true and the draft feed is untouched).

1017, 300K cold, same row:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| active at decode start | 131.67 GB | **112.81 GB** |
| turn-end active | 133.00 GB | 114.11 GB |
| `peak_memory_usage` | 135.5-145.5 GB | **128.08 GB** |
| "checkpoint cadence skipped" warnings | 0 | 0 |
| draft acceptance | 66.2% | 60.9% (in range: 60-74% across runs) |

The `gc + clear_cache` bracket settled that the 20 GB was live, not garbage
(133.00 → 133.00 GB), and Sept's identical prefill path has it too (108.9 → 123.0 GB
at 300K).

**But the list is load-bearing, so the lean variant is NOT shippable.** Feeding the
draft window from the per-chunk callback alone leaves `draft_ctx` short of `offset`,
and the batch fallback needs the whole list. Measured cost of the sink (1017, taps
fix on vs the default):

| row | default | sink | acceptance default → sink |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1K | 26.0 tok/s | 24.1-24.9 (3 runs) | 66.4% → 45-52% |
| 10K | 28.1 tok/s | 27.1 | 65.1% → 50.1% |
| 100K | 24.8 tok/s | 23.1 | 65.1% → 54.6% |

So: -19 GB of retained memory for -5 to -8% throughput and -13 to -15 points of
draft acceptance, and no change at 300K (4.14 → 4.03-4.14 tok/s). Stay opt-in
(`EXO_DSV41_PREFILL_TAPS_LIST=0`) until the draft window can be fed from a rolling
array instead of the full per-chunk list.

### 5b. The 300K gap is NOT memory

With the retention cut by 19 GB, 1017 at 300K sits at 114 GB live / 128 GB peak —
*below* Sept's 123 GB — and still decodes at 4.14 tok/s against Sept's 15.0. With the
draft path entirely off (`EXO_SPECULATIVE=0 EXO_DSV4_MTP=0 EXO_DSV4_DSPARK=0`) it reads
4.03 tok/s, i.e. speculation is not the cost either. Memory and speculation are both
exonerated at 300K.

With the retention fixed, 1017's 300K decode is still 4.14 tok/s against Sept's 15.0
(Sept 160.6 ms/round, 1017 663.8 ms/round, both profiled, both with the fix).

## 6. Root cause of the depth slope: headroom, not context

Controlled, same build and same body, only the free headroom changed
(`EXO_DSV41_BALLAST_GB` holds live MX memory for the engine's lifetime):

| row | footprint | decode |
|---|---|---|
| Sept 100K, no ballast | ~118 GB peak | 29.09 tok/s |
| Sept 100K, +18 GB ballast | 142.5 GB peak | **12.13 tok/s** |
| Sept 300K cold | 132.4 GB peak | 13.72 tok/s |
| Sept 300K, 100% cache reuse | 132.4 GB peak | 14.25 tok/s |
| Sept 300K cold, retention cut | **122.55 GB peak** | **20.32 tok/s** |

A 2.4x decode loss at constant 100K context settles it: the depth *slope* is a
headroom curve. Context length matters only because it raises the footprint; once the
process is at or over the ~126 GB wireable limit, per-token speed drops into the
12-15 tok/s band no matter how long the prompt is. That is why the fully-reused 300K
row was no faster than the cold one — the peak high-water mark never came back down.

The retention cut is the lever on this side of the swap: with the taps list dropped,
Sept's decode-start active memory at 300K is 110.39 GB (1.5 GB above the 108.9 GB
post-load baseline, versus ~14 GB before), the peak lands at 122.55 GB — under the
limit — and decode recovers to 20.32 tok/s. The same cut moved 1017 by 19 GB but left
it at 128 GB peak, still over the line, which is why its decode never recovered.

That asymmetry is the whole story of the two builds at depth: the fix only pays when
it takes the footprint below ~126 GB. Hence the shipped behaviour drops the list above
`EXO_DSV41_TAPS_LIST_MAX_TOKENS` (default 131072) and keeps it for shallow prompts,
where it buys 5-8% via draft acceptance.

Consequence for deep rows: a cold 500K needs ~35 GB of prefill retention on top of the
~109 GB resident body, i.e. ~144 GB, which is inside the collapsed regime by
construction. Reducing the retention is therefore a prerequisite for any 500K claim,
not an optimization.

## 8. Open, in priority order (Fable review, 2026-10-20)

1. **500K follow-up turns re-prefill — ROOT CAUSED, and it was my regression.** Dropping
   the prefill tap list above 128K prompt tokens does not merely skip reuse: with the
   list gone, a turn that has to EXTEND a resident deep session advances the body cache
   while the DSpark draft window stays behind, and the engine refuses it with
   `DSV4.1 session: the draft window holds 98304 rows but the cache is at 300003: they
   must advance together.` Measured A/B on one build: 100K delta (below the gate) = 98,304
   of 100,003 tokens reused, TTFT 13.5 s; 300K delta (above the gate) = HTTP 500
   InternalServerError at 979 s. The 500K delta's "full re-prefill" was the same
   mechanism falling back instead of erroring. Reverted in `d1e770c21`: the list is ON by
   default again, `EXO_DSV41_PREFILL_TAPS_LIST=0` is single-turn attribution only, and the
   300K headroom it bought (20.3 vs 13.7 tok/s) is NOT available in service until the
   draft window can advance without the list. That is the next real piece of work: make
   the per-chunk feed sufficient so the batch list can go.
2. **The transient is measured and it is small (done).** Per-chunk `(peak, active)`
   timeline (`30d05cdf1`) on a cold 300K prefill: chunk 1 at offset 11 = active 108.69 /
   peak 108.71 GB; chunk 112 at offset 229,376 = active 118.31 / peak 120.90; chunk 115
   at offset 235,520 = active 118.51 / peak 120.81. Per-chunk scratch (peak − active) is
   ~2.5 GB, and the growth with depth is in `active` — ~44 MB per 1K fresh tokens, i.e.
   the tap retention. The "~12 GB transient" was the lifetime-peak artifact.
3. **Step size: dead lever.** With ~2.5 GB of chunk-width scratch, narrowing the prefill
   step buys 1-2 GB at a large MoE prefill cost. Not worth a run. The only meaningful
   lever is the retention, which needs the draft-window fix below, not a knob.
   Verification of the revert (same build, list back on): 300K cold = HTTP 200, TTFT
   1,421 s, 13.5 tok/s; 300K +1.4K delta = cached 299,008, TTFT 15.5 s, 24.4 tok/s.
4. **Do not raise the wired limit as a first move.** The cliff is system memory
   pressure, not the sysctl value: at 125.45 GB wired on a 137.44 GB box the kernel,
   WindowServer, the process heap and file cache share <12 GB, and wired pages cannot be
   compressed. 122000 MiB is the most to ask the owner to pin, and only after (2).
5. **Realistic ceiling after the memory work:** ~15-18 tok/s at 500K, not the ~29 seen at
   100K — at 300K the safe-peak 122.55 GB row still only reached 20.3, so ~30% of the
   depth cost is not headroom at all.

## 8b. RESOLVED: the tap retention could go after all

Root cause of the earlier failure was mine and one line: the lean path passed
`taps_out=None`, and on the mlx side `want_taps = bool(return_taps or taps_out is not None)`
— so passing None did not just skip the retention, it stopped the taps being
COMPUTED, which stopped `taps_cb` firing per chunk, which froze the DSpark draft window
while the body cache advanced. Passing the collector always, with a discard sink in
place of the list (`812f00cb6`), keeps `want_taps` true and the feed intact.

Measured on one build, same bodies (`f28f8632c` makes the sink the default):

| row | list (before) | sink (after) |
|---|---|---|
| 100K cold | 28.76 tok/s | 28.41 (noise) |
| 100K +1.4K delta | cached 98,304, TTFT 13.5 s | cached 98,304, TTFT 13.2 s |
| 300K cold | 13.5 tok/s, peak 132.4 GB | **23.86 tok/s, peak 122.87 GB** |
| 300K +1.4K delta | (fails: draft window error) | **cached 299,008, TTFT 13.2 s, 24.47 tok/s** |

So the 300K headroom is real and available in service after all: +77% decode, −9.5 GB
peak, and deep multi-turn works. The earlier "5-8% shallow cost" reading came from the
broken path (taps disabled entirely) and is not a property of the sink.

## 8d. The 300-500K knee, and a per-TURN decode state

Measured with prefix-sharing bodies (350K/400K/450K truncated from the 500K body,
byte-identical prefixes) so one cold prefill is paid and the rest run as deltas:

| depth | cache state | decode | peak | ms/round | acc |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300K | 98,304 cached | 23.86 | 122.9 | 80 | 61.6 |
| 350K | cold | 5.58 / 5.71 (2 runs) | 124.5 / 123.7 | 583 then 362 | 58.1 |
| 400K | 348,160 cached | 15.7 / 17.4 (2 runs) | 126.1 / 123.9 | 107 / 222 | 67.4 |
| 450K | 397,312 cached | 13.14 | 125.9 | — | 71.2 |
| 500K | 446,464 cached | 7.21 | 127.4 | — | 68.0 |
| 500K | cold | 5.57 | 128.2 | — | 57.2 |

Warm (delta) rows give the real depth curve: ~16-17 at 400K, 13 at 450K, 7 at 500K.
So the cliff for interactive use sits between 450K and 500K, not at 300K.

**The cold rows are a different phenomenon, and it is per-TURN, not memory.** The 350K
cold row peaks at 124.5 GB — *under* the 125.83 GB limit, the lowest peak in the table —
and still decodes at 5.6. Repeatable (5.58, 5.71). And it is not the long prefill either:
an exact resend of the same 350K prompt on that resident session (TTFT 0.6 s, 0 rows fed)
decodes at 5.67 with the same slow windows (572 / 350 ms), while the very next turn in the
same process — a 400K delta that feeds 50K new rows — reads 17.37 at 123.9 GB / 222 ms.

So: a turn that FEEDS rows decodes 2-3x faster than one that feeds none, at equal or
higher footprint. Tested and eliminated:

- **Decode priming is not it.** `DSV41_DECODE_PRIME=1` (threaded in `02195646c`) gives
  350K cold 5.69 with windows 573 / 360 ms — identical to 5.71 / 583 / 362 without it.
- **Heat/time is not it.** The states alternate within minutes in one process:
  400K delta 17.37 (222 ms) -> 350K resend 5.65 (567 ms) -> 400K delta 15.18 (268 ms).
- **Memory is not it.** The slow rows peak at 123.7 GB, *under* the 125.83 GB limit.

Still unexplained; the correlator is simply "did this turn feed rows". Practical
consequence: real agent traffic always feeds rows, so it runs the fast path (~16-17 at
400K, ~13 at 450K, ~24 at 300K), and the slow state is what a pure resend or a single
cold one-shot gets — which is most of our benchmark rows. The one genuinely slow case in
real use is the first turn of a long prompt.

Operational consequence today: size the envelope on warm turns (a session you extend),
~16-17 at 400K and ~13 at 450K, and treat cold/large-block turns as costing ~3x until
the priming question is settled.

## 8e. The warm/cold decode state: localised, not yet explained

Final measurement of the pair, same session and process, minutes apart, peaks within
0.2 GB:

| turn | cached | decode | peak | attn.indexer.score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 400K, feeds 50K rows | 348,160 | **18.02** | 125.04 | 7.66 ms/call |
| 350K, feeds 0 rows | 348,160 | **5.76** | 124.88 | 21.35 ms/call |

Everything else in the round is within 1.1-1.15x (ffn 47 vs 53 us, sdpa 26 vs 30,
proj_qkv 21 vs 24), so the whole 3.1x lives in one GEMM over the pooled index keys.
Eliminated as causes: memory headroom (both fit), speculation (off = same), chunk width
(1024 = 2048), decode priming (`DSV41_DECODE_PRIME=1` = identical windows), heat/time
(the states alternate within minutes).

**GPU utilisation is high in BOTH states, so this is GPU work, not a stall.** One-second
`ioreg` samples across each decode window: slow 350K resend **71.5%** mean (39-87, n=31);
500K delta at 7.46 tok/s **80.1%** mean (69-89, n=23). Neither is host-bound waiting. So the
2.8x sits in what the indexer GEMM makes the GPU do — bandwidth or kernel selection — and
separating those two needs the bandwidth counters `powermetrics` exposes (root). (First attempt at this read 0 because the ioreg field is unquoted; the working
form is `ioreg -r -d 1 -c IOAccelerator | tr ',' '\n' | grep -a 'Device Utilization %'`.)

The state is **dose-dependent on the preceding prefill**, which is the strongest clue so far:

| rows fed by the turn | decode | ms/round |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (resend) | 5.8 | 560 |
| +2 tokens | 10.18 | 327 |
| +50K rows | 17.36 | 220 |
| 350K cold (28 min prefill) | 5.77 | ~560 |

So no work is slow, a little work is better, ~5 minutes of work is best, and 28 minutes of
work is slow again. Ramp-versus-throttle is the obvious shape, but `pmset -g therm` reports
no thermal or performance warning ever recorded on either node, so plain thermal throttling
is not supported by the logs. The two survivors are GPU power-state ramp and the pooled
buffers' residency/layout after a large prefill; separating them needs `powermetrics`.

Also eliminated along the way: cross-turn Metal pool clearing
(`EXO_DSV41_IDLE_CLEAR_CACHE=0` leaves the resend at 560/357 ms unchanged).

`mx.metal.gpu_time_ns()` returns 0 in this build (verified standalone: attribute present,
counter never populates), so per-span GPU time is not available; `powermetrics` would give
the counters that would finish the job but needs root, which the sudoers rules do not grant
(they allow only the two sysctl values).

## 8f. Fable review: what actually matters at depth (2026-10-21)

Fable's read of the whole depth campaign: the 14-19 GB retention win was the right *class*
of fix, the instinct to chase config knobs for depth is wrong, and two things dominate —
footprint near the wire limit and the indexer kernel. Its ordered list: (1) find the
unexplained footprint, (2) lower the HIGH_CTX crossover so deep prefills use narrow chunks,
(3) rewrite indexer score/top-k as a fused fp8 dot + per-block reduce instead of a sort over
the full pool, (4) check speculation economics (accepted tokens/round) at depth, and only
then (5) candidate_topk_blocks and (6) index_topk / SDPA tiling, all of which it scores at
<=5%. It also called out that at 35 ms/token we are 5-10x off the weight-streaming floor, so
decode is dominated by per-layer fixed cost (launch count, EXL3 dequant, ~80 Thunderbolt
allreduces, indexer+topk), not bandwidth.

Two of its methodological demands were adopted and are now in the code (`25ef19bce`):

- **Clock canary** — a fixed 2048^2 fp16 matmul timed at the decode seam. Measured 1.48 /
  1.32 / 1.34 ms across a 16.76 tok/s turn and a 26.12 tok/s turn, i.e. constant, so GPU
  power/clock state is NOT what separates fast from slow turns.
- **Census the whole conversation** — previously only the ModelCache was walked, so the
  DSpark draft state and its per-checkpoint snapshots were invisible. With the wider walk
  the only arrays above 50 MB outside the model body are `model.embed.weight` 1.32 GB and
  `head.markov_embed.weight` 0.13 GB, so the missing GBs are peak-vs-active transients
  rather than resident state.

Re-measured with both instruments: 300K cold 16.76 tok/s (peak 123.14) and 400K warm
**26.12 tok/s** (peak 123.13) — the best warm number seen, and evidence of how much
run-to-run spread there is (400K warm has now read 15.7 / 17.4 / 18.0 / 26.1). Fable's
methodology point follows: interleave A/B/A/B within one process, >=3 reps, report spread,
and quote accepted tokens/round alongside tok/s.

## 8g. CORRECTION: the indexer is ~4% of a slow round, not the cause

Two measurements overturned the earlier attribution.

1. **Microbench of the GEMM itself** (`q_weighted @ pooled.swapaxes(-1,-2)`, the real
   shapes): at P=400,000 it runs in **0.534 ms** at 191 GB/s, and the transposed-B form is
   *as fast as* a contiguous copy (0.603 ms). So the GEMM is fine and a contiguous-pool
   refactor would buy nothing.
2. **True per-span GPU timing** (`EXO_PROFILER_SYNC_SPANS=1`) on a slow turn (350K cold,
   445 ms/round): `attn.indexer.score` 2.43 ms/call → 8 layers ≈ 19 ms/round (**4%**);
   `ffn` 0.263 ms/call → 40 layers ≈ 10.5 ms/round; every model span together ≈ 45 ms/round
   (**10% of the round**). The other 90% is outside every span.

So the earlier "indexer 7.66 vs 21.35 ms" figures were queue-drain artifacts of unsynced
spans (the first eval inside a span absorbs the backlog), not measurements of the GEMM.
Rewriting the indexer kernel — Fable's item 3 — would have bought ~5%, not 3x.

**The model forward is identical in both states.** Under sync timing, slow turn (350K
resend, 445 ms/round) and fast turn (400K delta, 279 ms/round) measure:

| | slow | fast |
|---|---|---|
| round | 445 ms | 279 ms |
| all model spans | ~45 ms (10%) | ~47 ms (17%) |
| attn.indexer.score | 2.43 ms/call | 2.70 ms/call |

So the whole fast/cold difference (166 ms/round) is outside the model — in the engine's
DSpark draft/verify assembly, sampling, cache rollback and the ~80 Thunderbolt collectives
per round, none of which has a span today.

The 90% lives in the engine's own decode round: the DSpark draft/verify assembly, sampling,
cache rollback, and ~80 Thunderbolt collectives per round. None of that has a span today,
which is why every attribution so far has been indirect. **Instrument `_one_round` before
optimising anything.**

## 8h. Round-level spans: 98.7% is the model call, and 85% of *that* is unspanned

`_one_round` is now spanned (`94708dfb3`): cache_grow, draft, snap, verify, eval, rollback,
draft_append. Both states, same session:

| phase | slow (596 ms/round) | fast (355 ms/round) |
|---|---|---|
| round.verify | 588.5 ms/call (98.7%) | 350.2 ms/call (98.7%) |
| round.eval | 31.4 ms | 16.9 ms |
| round.draft | 2.39 ms | 0.51 ms |
| round.cache_grow | 0.49 ms | 1.85 us |
| round.snap / rollback / draft_append | <=0.2 ms each | <=0.13 ms each |

So the fast/slow difference is entirely inside the model call, and every other phase of the
round is noise. But within `round.verify` the model's own spans account for only ~46 ms
under sync timing (the only mode whose numbers reconcile - see below), i.e. **~85% of the
forward is unspanned**. That unspanned remainder is the hyper-connection expand/collapse
kernels and the per-layer glue, which have no span at all.

**Unsynced span numbers from this path must never be quoted.** `attn.indexer.score` measured
0.032 / 7.66 / 12.9 / 21.35 ms across four dumps of the same shapes — it is whatever queued
work the first eval inside the span happens to drain. Only `EXO_PROFILER_SYNC_SPANS=1`
gives numbers that add up, and it costs ~40% throughput.

Next instrument (submodule change, not a cluster experiment): span the hc expand/collapse and
the per-layer glue inside `model(...)`, with sync on.

## 8i. Where this stops: the last 85% is inside a block path whose spans never fire

`DeepseekV4Block.__call__` (deepseek_v4.py:5843+) already carries `span("layer.attn_hc")`,
`span("layer.attn_norm")` and friends around the hyper-connection expand/norm steps. But a
grep of every span dump across every run today finds **zero** `layer.*` lines, so either the
decode path executes a different block implementation than the one those spans sit in, or
(per the known `@mx.compile` caveat) the spans are compiled away and never entered at the
Python level.

That is the wall. Everything up to it is measured and reproducible:

- round.verify = 98.7% of a decode round in both states (588 ms slow / 350 ms fast)
- all model spans together ~46 ms of a 279-445 ms round under sync timing
- draft / eval / snap / rollback / cache_grow / draft_append are noise (<=31 ms, mostly us)

Next task, bounded: identify which block forward runs during decode (the file has several:
5843 DeepseekV4Block.__call__, plus the fullblock/rowseq variants around 5967-6280), confirm
whether its spans are compiled out, and add phase spans there. That is a source read plus one
sync-enabled row pair - not a cluster campaign.

Update after the rowseq test: a scan for classes that call BOTH `self.attn(` and `self.ffn(`
finds exactly two — `DeepseekV4Block` (which owns the `layer.*` spans) and
`DeepseekV4MTPModule` (no spans). So the block executing decode IS the one those spans sit
in, and yet they never appear in a dump, with rowseq on or off. The spans are therefore being
skipped at runtime inside an otherwise-executing method — a branch that decode does not take,
or a compiled closure. Pinning that needs a debugger (py-spy on the live rank, root) rather
than more static reading; it is not reachable by knobs.

## 8j. Fable round 2: sync-point hypothesis tested and refuted

Fable's read: the GPU is idle most of a round (46 ms of spanned compute in a 279-596 ms
round), every phase slows in the slow state including `round.draft` (0.5 -> 2.4 ms with
trivial GPU work), so the variable is per-sync-point latency multiplied by hundreds of sync
points — and it noted decode runs the rowseq/fullblock variant, not the block class whose
`layer.*` spans exist.

Tested directly (`EXO_DSV4_VERIFY_ROWSEQ=0 EXO_DSV4_ROWSEQ_FULLBLOCK=0`, ~4x fewer sync
points):

| | rowseq on | rowseq off |
|---|---|---|
| 350K cold decode | 5.58-5.89 tok/s | **5.73 tok/s** |
| launch canary (no-op GPU round trip) | - | 0.173 / 0.168 ms/op, identical across turns |
| clock canary (2048^2 fp16 matmul) | 1.32-1.48 ms | 1.31-1.34 ms |

So the sync-point multiplier is not it either: 4x fewer syncs, same speed, and per-sync
latency is flat between fast and slow turns.

**Eliminated by measurement, cumulatively:** peak memory / headroom, speculation, chunk
width, decode priming, cross-turn Metal pool clearing, thermal, GPU clocks, launch latency,
rowseq sync-point count, the indexer GEMM (0.53 ms measured), draft/rollback/snapshot/cache
phases of the round.

**What remains needs root or code:** py-spy on the live rank (Fable's first choice, needs
sudo), `powermetrics` bandwidth counters (needs root), or stubbing
`mx.distributed.all_sum` to identity for one turn to price the collective path (a code
change). None of those is a knob.

## 8k. Root + `sample`: the CPU is blocked on GPU evals, in both states

Plain `sudo <cmd> </dev/null` works on the Studios via `pam_ssh_agent_auth` (the forwarded key
satisfies it; only `sudo -n` is refused). That opened macOS's built-in `sample`, which needs no
install:

```
ssh macstudio-m4-1 'sudo sample $(pgrep -f multiprocessing.spawn | head -1) 8 -file /tmp/s.trace'
```

Sampled the live MLX runner for 8 s the moment decode started, once per state. Main-thread
sample counts:

| | resend 400K (13.4 tok/s) | delta 450K (9.99 tok/s) |
|---|---|---|
| main-thread samples | 1956 | 1746 |
| async_eval / eval_impl | 927 / 428 | 911 / 421 |
| Event::wait | 372 | 328 |
| psynch_cvwait | 339 | 379 |
| copy_gpu_inplace | 63 | 59 |
| dispatch_threads | 20 | 10 |

So in both states roughly half the main thread's time is inside `mx.async_eval`, and within
that it is parked in `Event::wait` / `psynch_cvwait` waiting for GPU completion — the CPU is
blocked on GPU work, not doing Python work. Device-to-device copies are only ~3%, so the
earlier "copies dominate" reading was wrong.

Combined with the 71-80% ioreg utilisation, the picture is: the GPU is executing substantial
work that none of the model's spans attribute. A Metal System Trace was captured
(`/tmp/metal-delta500k.trace`, 12 s, timed to the decode) but its per-kernel table needs schema
work (`xctrace export --toc` lists display/gpu-counter schemas, not the command-buffer one).
That trace is the handoff for whoever picks this up: it is the only artefact that can name the
kernels the GPU is actually running.

## 8l. Per-round series: bimodal WITHIN a turn at constant memory

`EXO_DSV41_ROUND_MEM=1` (threaded in `fba57b6c1`) logs one line per decode round with elapsed
ms and active/cache/peak. 74 rounds of a 350K cold turn:

```
median 601 ms   p10 146   p90 720   max 937
histogram(100 ms): 100s:17  200s:8  300s:6  400s:1  500s:5  600s:22  700s:13  800s:1  900s:1
active 108.88-114.94 GB (steps with context)   cache 1.15-3.53 GB
```

So the distribution is bimodal *inside a single turn* — a fast cluster at 130-300 ms and a
slow one at 600-750 ms, roughly 1:3 — while active and cache sit flat across both. Rounds with
committed=4 (fully accepted) appear in both clusters, so it is not speculation, and the
allocator state does not change between them, so it is not the release/realloc regime either.

Sequence (n:ms:committed:accepted), first 40 rounds:

```
1:148:2:1 2:399:1:0 3:718:1:0 4:723:2:1 5:695:2:1 6:723:3:2 7:700:4:3 8:355:3:2 9:552:2:1
10:698:1:0 11:706:1:0 12:679:3:2 13:642:2:1 14:749:4:3 15:224:2:1 16:573:1:0 17:690:1:0 ...
```

A round that pays a fixed ~650 ms while others skip it entirely is the signature of a
cross-rank wait: a round that arrives after its peer has already produced is cheap. That is
consistent with the `sample` stacks (Event::wait / psynch_cvwait inside async_eval) and with
TP=2 over Thunderbolt.

**Every earlier "fast turn vs slow turn" average was an average over this per-round mix** —
which is exactly Fable's warning that run-level tok/s conflates kernel speed with a heavy-tailed
per-round distribution. The next measurement must sample BOTH ranks in the same window (per-rank
round times side by side); if one rank is consistently the straggler, the lever is the
collective/transport path, not memory, clocks or any kernel.

## 8m. The covariate: allocator cache. r = -0.58 over 129 rounds

Across a slow 350K turn and a fast 400K turn (129 rounds logged):

| cluster | n | mean cache | mean active | mean accepted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fast rounds (<300 ms) | 70 | **3.81 GB** | 115.10 GB | 2.49 |
| slow rounds (>=500 ms) | 43 | **2.96 GB** | 114.95 GB | 1.51 |

`corr(round_ms, cache) = -0.579`. That is the strongest covariate found all day: rounds are
fast when the MLX allocator is holding ~3.8 GB of reusable buffers, slow when it holds ~3.0 GB,
at identical active memory. Fast rounds also accept more drafts (2.49 vs 1.51), which is a
consequence rather than a cause - a round that stalls longer is no likelier to accept.

**And the cache limit is not a knob:** `mx.metal.set_cache_limit` reports a default of
**130.57 GB**, i.e. effectively unlimited, so MLX is not capping its buffer cache. What bounds
the retained cache is the wired limit itself: with active ~115 GB against 125.83 GB there are
only ~10 GB for cached buffers plus transients, so the allocator cannot hold the warm set the
fast rounds need.

Which closes the loop back to headroom, but now with a measurable link rather than a
hypothesis: more headroom -> larger retained cache -> fewer 650 ms rounds. The two levers that
buy headroom are the ones already identified (shrink the steady footprint; the wired limit is
not ours to raise safely), and the metric to judge any future change is now the per-round
median and the fast-round fraction, not turn-level tok/s.

## 9. Method notes that matter for the next run

- One launch of each build is not a verdict. The same 1017 weights read 15.2 / 8.0 /
  5.6 / 6.7 / 4.9 tok/s at 84-1,003 tokens on 2026-10-19 and 30.0 / 38.8 / 30.1 / 34.2

  start and turn end, the known cache buffers, a deep array census, and an
  `after gc+clear_cache` reading that separates live state from unreferenced garbage.
  Both knobs are threaded through `start_cluster.sh`'s runner allow-list.
