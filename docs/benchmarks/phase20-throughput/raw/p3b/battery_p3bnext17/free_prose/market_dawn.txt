# market_dawn [prose]
PROMPT: Describe a street market at dawn in vivid sensory detail, at least 150 words.

Dawn arrives not as a sudden brightness but as a slow bleed of gray into indigo. The street market wakes in whispers and clatter. Metal shutters rattle up like teeth. Vendors haul crates of oranges, their skins still cool and beaded with mist. The air smells of wet cardboard, diesel, crushed mint, and the sweet yeast of flatbread baking in a tandoor. A butcher sharpens a knife with a rhythmic hiss. Somewhere a radio plays tinny devotional music. Puddles reflect strings of bare bulbs, each a tiny moon. The ground is slick with hose water and trampled leaves. A woman arranges tomatoes into pyramids, her fingers stained red. Steam rises from a cauldron of chickpeas, spiced and cumin-heavy, wrapping around shoulders. The first customers shuffle in, yawning, clutching cloth bags. A flower stall bursts with marigolds, their scent thick as honey. The sky turns rose-gold. Voices overlap: prices, jokes, the slap of fish on ice. By the time sun touches rooftops, the market is a loud, breathing thing.
