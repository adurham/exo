### CONTRADICTIONS (do not smooth over)

**C1 — item (i) and (ii) do not agree on what "per layer per token" means, and neither literal reading survives contact with the real per-token budget.**
Taking (i)'s 2935.64ms envelope literally as "summed over 256 output tokens" and dividing by 43 gives 266.7us/layer/token — **5.25x smaller** than (ii)'s real, independently-measured ~1400us/layer. Taking (i) instead as a *single call's* envelope (the TL;DR's "GPU idle >99% of the time" framing reads this way — one `switch_mlp` invocation stalling for ~2.9 seconds) makes it **~2000x larger** than (ii)'s per-layer figure. Neither interpretation reconciles with the real number. The honest conclusion: **the 2935ms figure cannot be safely divided by 256 or by 43 to produce a citable per-token or per-layer value — the source document does not establish which regime it is in, and both plausible readings fail an external consistency check.**

**C2 — item (ii), read literally as "per output token," is impossible; it is actually a per-verify-cycle figure, and the round1/mid-report framing that led to this task's brief risks re-committing exactly the error `PERFORMANCE_HISTORY.md` already retracted once.**
x43 = 60.2ms. At 37 t/s aggregate (c=2, both streams), the real per-token interval is 27.03ms. **60.2ms cannot be the cost of producing one token** — it is 2.23x the entire aggregate per-token budget. This is not a new bug: `PERFORMANCE_HISTORY.md:570-597` already documents and retracts the *exact same class of error* for a related all_sum number — "43 layers × the sync-span-measured ~4094µs/call average = 176ms/token predicted, vs. 53.48ms/token real measured wall time — a 3.29x impossibility, since a per-call cost cannot exceed the total per-token budget it's supposedly a component of." **The 1400us figure is correctly a per-layer, per-verify-forward-pass cost** (one MTP verify cycle, which the primary source itself reconciles: "Sum of per-layer p50s = 59.68ms (close to FENCE=43 verify mean 57ms)" — `.hermes/plans/2026-05-19_allsum_tail_findings.md:71`, and round1 independently anchors "56ms verify" — `REPORT.md:23,93`). Because DSv4 runs speculative decode (γ=2 MTP), one verify cycle yields on the order of ~2 output tokens (not 1), so **any verdict that cites "~1400us × 43 = 60ms" as "the cost of one decode token" is quoting it in the wrong regime by roughly a factor of 2** (tokens/cycle) on top of not even being the dominant per-cycle cost driver (draft + accept add ~5.3ms more, per the same source, lines 134-136). **SAY THIS PLAINLY: any verdict must cite ~1400us/layer as "per verify-forward-pass," never as "per output token."**

**C3 — item (iii)'s mechanism, honestly extrapolated to the real decode payload, explains at most ~7% of item (ii)'s real per-layer cost, not the bulk of it.**
The 2.66x stream-boundary/coherency mechanism is real and independently reproduced (phase0a doc, zero-wire-bytes control), and its payload-linearity (~7 GB/s) is solid across a 64x payload sweep. But extrapolated down to the actual decode payload (32 KiB, not 16.8 MB), it predicts **~5-100us per layer** (pure-linear: 4.68us; floor+linear: ~100us) against a real measured **~1400us/layer**. Even the generous floor+linear estimate accounts for only **~7%** of the real per-layer cost. **This is a genuine finding that should be stated plainly in any verdict: the phase0a stream-boundary mechanism, while real at prefill-scale (MB) payloads, is NOT sufficient to explain the decode-scale (32 KiB) per-layer all_sum drain — roughly 93% of the ~1400us remains attributable to something else** (consistent with round1's own conclusion that ~95% of the 1400us budget is local GPU drain/dispatch overhead unrelated to the collective's peer-wait or wire cost — `REPORT.md:19-20`, `I1-COLLECTIVE-LATENCY.md:192`).

**C4 — item (iv)'s "~7ms real GPU compute" was measured with the SAME instrumentation technique `PERFORMANCE_HISTORY.md` later documents as producing misattributed cost, and its absolute value should not be trusted.**
`docs/dsv4-decode-stall-2026-06-26.md`'s entire profile (items i and iv both) comes from `EXO_PROFILER_SYNC_SPANS=1`. `PERFORMANCE_HISTORY.md:582-597` (2026-08-22 entry) explicitly retracts an earlier, analogous sync-span-derived all_sum number using this reasoning: *"`mx.synchronize()` at a span boundary drains MLX's ENTIRE pending lazy graph since the last sync point (not just the spanned op), so a span ending right after `all_sum` misattributes real upstream GPU-compute time from prior layers to the collective specifically."* This is the identical measurement technique used to produce the June 26 doc's `~2.3ms per gather_qmm` / `~7ms MoE compute` numbers. Independently, round1 (2026-09-03, corrected chained-graph methodology) measures the same op class (gather_qmm, M=4 verify shape) at **350.3us/call** — **6.6x smaller** than the June doc's 2.3ms. Trying the alternative "summed over 256-token run" reading (forced by item i's percentage math) instead gives an implausible ~0.5us/call, which is physically too small for a real GEMM dispatch and contradicts round1's own dispatch-floor finding of ~92-97us. **Neither reading of "~7ms"/"~2.3ms each" survives cross-check against round1's controlled measurement. The number should be cited only as directional support for ">99% of the switch_mlp envelope is stall, not compute" (a ratio, internally self-consistent regardless of the per-call-vs-summed ambiguity) — never as an absolute GPU-compute-time figure.**

**Bottom line for whoever writes the verdict:** items (i) and (iv) came from one instrumentation pass (`EXO_PROFILER_SYNC_SPANS=1`, 2026-06-26) whose absolute-time outputs are the *exact class* of artifact `PERFORMANCE_HISTORY.md` has already caught and retracted once for a sibling number. Only their **qualitative claim** ("switch_mlp span is >99% GPU idle, not compute") is corroborated by later, cleaner measurements (round1's local-GPU-drain finding, ~95% of the 1400us budget). Item (ii)'s ~1400us is real and reconciles cleanly with the record **only when cited as "per verify-forward-pass," not "per output token."** Item (iii) is real but, extrapolated honestly, is nowhere near large enough to be the dominant explanation for item (ii).

## GENUINELY UNTESTED HYPOTHESES

- **H-e** — GPU-resident/Metal-kernel collective. Zero implementation attempts found; only an architectural fact ("no `eval_gpu` for all_sum") is on record.
- **H-f** — Fusing/coalescing the 43 per-layer collectives, or shrinking the per-layer payload via a sharding-scheme change. Named once as a theoretical future option in 2026-05-19, never attempted.
- **H-c** (as literally specified) — deliberately overlapping layer N's collective with layer N+1's compute. A related incidental-pipelining-benefit measurement exists (2026-08-29), but the specific cross-layer overlap mechanism was never implemented or benchmarked, and the one directly relevant precedent (same-layer overlap, H-b) broke quality hard — so this is untested AND flagged as hazardous to attempt naively.

H-a, H-b, and H-d are NOT untested — H-a and H-b both have documented negative-or-mixed results with specific numbers and dates; H-d is shipped but scoped to prefill, leaving its decode-specific applicability as the only open sub-question (not "untested," but "untested in the decode regime").
