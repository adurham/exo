# pencil_history [prose]
PROMPT: Write about the history of the pencil, at least 200 words.

The history of the pencil reaches back centuries before the familiar wooden object we know. Ancient Egyptians and Romans wrote with metal styluses, and later cultures used charcoal or lead, though true graphite was not yet part of the story. The modern pencil began in the 1560s, when a large, unusually pure deposit was discovered in Borrowdale, England. Legend says local shepherds found the black substance to mark sheep. It was soft, left dark marks, and was soon wrapped in string, leather, or wood to keep hands clean. Because Borrowdale graphite was so pure and valuable, England guarded the mine closely. By the 17th century, German craftsmen in Nuremberg were gluing graphite between wooden slats, creating early mass-produced pencils.

In 1795, during the Napoleonic Wars, French inventor Nicolas-Jacques Conté developed a method of mixing powdered graphite with clay, then shaping and baking it, which made graphite usable even when pure supplies ran low and allowed different grades of dark and hard lead. In 1812, William Munroe began making pencils in Massachusetts, and the Thoreau family in Concord later improved graphite processing. In 1858, Hymen Lipman patented the attached pencil eraser. In 1861, Eberhard Faber built a pencil factory in the United States. Mechanical pencils appeared in 1822 with Sampson Mordan and Gabriel Riddle, while Tokuji Hayakawa created the Ever-Ready Sharp in 1915.

Pencils became cheap, reliable tools for writing, drawing, and drafting. Grades like H, B, and HB standardized hardness. Today billions are sold yearly. Modern “lead” is really graphite and clay, not lead metal. The pencil’s history shows how a simple discovery became an everyday technology of communication.
