# market_dawn [prose]
PROMPT: Describe a street market at dawn in vivid sensory detail, at least 150 words.

The market at first light is a city learning to breathe. A bruised sky bleeds peach and iron over the rooftops while the cobblestones still hold last night’s rain, slick underfoot, cold enough to sting bare ankles. Vendors arrive in layers of wool and steam: bare bulbs overhead buzz like trapped wasps, casting yellow pools on crates of blood oranges, knobby ginger, bruised basil. A fishmonger slaps silver scales into a tray; the sound is wet, sharp, final. Somewhere a hand-cart rattles over stone, and metal shutters grind upward like yawns. Smells collide—brine and diesel, crushed mint, coriander, yeast from a bread stall, the sweet rot of overripe mango, coffee boiling in a dented pot. A butcher saws through bone; sawdust drifts, catching in the throat. A woman in a headscarf arranges pyramids of lemons, each one polished with a rag. She calls out prices in a voice still rough from darkness. A customer answers, then laughs. The air tastes of salt and smoke and something green. Pigeons strut between feet, pecking at cabbage leaves. As the sun finally cracks the horizon, the market fully wakes, loud, vivid, alive—an orchestra of ordinary hunger tuning itself to the morning.
