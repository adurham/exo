# pencil_history [prose]
PROMPT: Write about the history of the pencil, at least 200 words.

The pencil has humble origins in ancient styluses, but its modern form emerged after a lucky discovery of graphite in Borrowdale, England, around 1564. Shepherds and locals found a black mineral that left marks on sheep and paper. Because it resembled lead, people called it “lead,” and the name “lead pencil” stuck even though graphite contains no lead. Early users wrapped graphite chunks in string, leather, or sheepskin. Before long, German craftsmen in Nuremberg began setting graphite into wooden holders, creating the first recognizable pencils in the 17th century. England guarded its Borrowdale graphite, making pencils scarce and expensive elsewhere.

In 1795, French inventor Nicolas-Jacques Conté changed everything. During a graphite shortage caused by war, he mixed powdered graphite with clay, shaped the mixture into rods, and fired them in a kiln. By varying the clay-to-graphite ratio, he could control hardness and darkness—the basis of modern pencil grades. In 1812, William Munroe established an early American pencil factory in Concord, Massachusetts, and the Thoreau family, including Henry David Thoreau, later improved pencil-making there. In 1822, Sampson Mordan and Gabriel Ruddle patented an early mechanical pencil. In 1858, Hymen Lipman patented the pencil with an attached eraser, though the design was later refined.

By the late 19th century, pencils were mass-produced. In 1890, the Austrian company L&C Hardtmuth popularized the yellow pencil with its Koh-i-Noor line, and yellow became a common pencil color. Today pencils are made from graphite, clay, wood, and sometimes erasers, with mechanical pencils offering reusable alternatives. Though digital tools dominate, the pencil remains a simple, reliable, and enduring writing instrument.
