# market_dawn [prose]
PROMPT: Describe a street market at dawn in vivid sensory detail, at least 150 words.

Dawn arrives not as a sudden burst of light but as a slow bruise of violets and roses over the rooftops. The street is already awake, though half-dazed: metal shutters rattle upward, wooden crates thud onto damp cobblestones, and canvas awns snap like sails. The air is cold and wet, smelling of rain, diesel, crushed mint, and the first bitter coffee steaming in a vendor’s dented urn. Under yellow bulbs that buzz faintly, pyramids of oranges glow, tomatoes shine like lacquered jewels, and bundles of cilantro release a green sharpness when a hand brushes past. A fishmonger hoses his stall, and the briny scent mixes with frying dough and cinnamon smoke. Sounds layer—cart wheels squeaking, a radio murmurs old songs, vendors call prices in hoarse, practiced voices. The cold mist touches cheeks; warmth from a grill reaches knuckles. A torn piece of warm bread tastes sweet and yeasty, with salt on the lips. Everything feels provisional and urgent: a city stretching before full sun.
