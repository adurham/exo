# tests_opinion [opinion]
PROMPT: Are standardized tests a good measure of intelligence? Write two paragraphs arguing a position.

Standardized tests are not a good standalone measure of intelligence because they capture only a narrow slice of mental ability. They reward speed, memorization of test formats, and test-taking strategy more than deep reasoning, creativity, curiosity, or practical problem-solving. A student who scores poorly may be an exceptional artist, mechanic, negotiator, or original thinker, while a high scorer may struggle with collaborative or hands-on challenges. These tests are also heavily shaped by socioeconomic status, language background, cultural knowledge, and access to tutoring, so they often measure preparation and privilege as much as raw cognitive capacity.

The strongest defense of standardized tests is that they correlate with academic performance and can predict school success, but that is not the same as measuring intelligence. Intelligence is multifaceted: it includes verbal, spatial, logical, emotional, social, and adaptive abilities, and no single timed exam can represent all of them. When standardized tests are used as gatekeepers for gifted programs, college admissions, or job hiring, they can mislabel capable people and narrow the definition of who is “smart.” Therefore, standardized tests are useful as one imperfect indicator, but they are not a good measure of true, broad intelligence unless combined with portfolios, projects, teacher observations, and performance-based assessments.
