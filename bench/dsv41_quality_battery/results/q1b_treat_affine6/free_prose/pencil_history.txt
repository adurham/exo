# pencil_history [prose]
PROMPT: Write about the history of the pencil, at least 200 words.

The pencil’s history begins long before the familiar yellow No. 2. Ancient Romans wrote with a stylus, a thin metal rod used to scratch marks into wax tablets. The word “pencil” itself comes from the Latin *penicillus*, meaning “little brush” or “little tail.” The modern pencil, however, depends on graphite, a form of carbon discovered in Borrowdale, Cumberland, England, around 1564. Shepherds reportedly used the strange black mineral to mark sheep, and early users wrapped graphite chunks in string or cloth. Because people mistook graphite for lead, the name “lead pencil” stuck, even though pencils contain no lead.

By the 17th century, German and Italian craftsmen were setting graphite into wooden holders, creating early wooden pencils. England controlled the finest graphite deposits, so other nations sought alternatives. In 1795, during the Napoleonic Wars, French inventor Nicolas-Jacques Conté mixed powdered graphite with clay, shaped the mixture into rods, and fired them in a kiln. This process allowed pencils to be made from lower-quality graphite and produced different hardness grades. It remains the basis of modern pencil manufacturing.

In the 19th century, pencil making spread. American Henry David Thoreau worked in his family’s pencil business and improved graphite grinding. In 1822, Sampson Mordan and John Isaac Hawkins patented an early mechanical pencil. In 1858, Hymen Lipman patented attaching an eraser to a pencil’s end. In 1889, the Austrian company Koh-i-Noor introduced a yellow pencil, and yellow became a popular symbol of quality. Today, pencils are graded from hard to soft, with HB and No. 2 among the most common. Though simple, the pencil is a product of centuries of innovation.
