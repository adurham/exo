# lighthouse [continuation]
PROMPT: Continue this story in one vivid paragraph: 'The lighthouse keeper noticed the light was not his own.'

It came from the sea—a second beam, thin and silver, sliding across the waves like a blade, then climbing the tower with the calm of something that knew exactly where he stood. His own lamp guttered as if ashamed, and the glass panes ticked with cold. Down in the cove, where no ship should be, a hull blacker than the night hung motionless, its masts bare, its decks crowded with lanterns that blinked in no rhythm he recognized. The keeper pressed his palm to the window and felt a vibration through the stone, a slow pulse like a heartbeat under the floor. Then the strange light swung toward him and stopped, and in its glare he saw, standing at the rail, a figure in an old-fashioned coat lifting a lamp in greeting—his own face, younger by forty winters, mouthing a word he could not hear. The keeper reached for the bell rope, but before he pulled, the sea-light answered his beam again, and every stair behind him filled with the smell of salt and burning oil, as if the tower had remembered another keeper, and was waiting.
