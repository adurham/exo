# market_dawn [prose]
PROMPT: Describe a street market at dawn in vivid sensory detail, at least 150 words.

At dawn, the street market wakes in layers of gray and gold. Streetlamps still buzz over cobblestones slick with mist, while the first sun splits between rooftops and lays long amber bars across crates of oranges. Vendors move like shadows: hauling canvas, snapping tarps, dragging coolers that scrape the ground. The air is a thick braid of smells—wet cardboard, diesel, crushed mint, brine, and yeast from a bakery whose shutters rattle open. Somewhere a radio plays tinny cumbia; a knife thwacks through melon; ice chips into a fishmonger’s tray with a sound like glass. Underfoot, the stone is cold and gritty. A flower stall spills buckets of carnations, their stems beaded with water. Coffee steam curls from a dented urn, bitter and dark. A vendor offers a slice of pineapple, cool and sharp on my tongue. Voices rise—“¡A peso, a peso!”—hoarse from sleep. Pigeons peck at a fallen crust. The market smells of possibility: fruit flesh, spice dust, damp earth, and the faint metallic tang of coins. By full morning it will roar, but now it is tender, busy, and alive.
