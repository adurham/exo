# lighthouse [continuation]
PROMPT: Continue this story in one vivid paragraph: 'The lighthouse keeper noticed the light was not his own.'

The lighthouse keeper noticed the light was not his own. It slid across the black water in a slow green sweep, too low for any ship, too steady for any buoy, and when it touched the cliff it left a shimmer like frost on the granite. His own lamp, a faithful yellow pulse he’d tended for thirty years, guttered and bowed as if ashamed; the brass rail hummed under his palms, and the fog tasted of iron and distant rain. Out beyond the reef where no rock should stand, a second tower rose from the sea—slim, dark, crowned with that cold emerald eye—and it began to answer him, not in the old rhythm he knew, but in a slow, deliberate code that spelled his name, letter by letter, then waited. He checked the wick, the oil, the clock, and found all three unchanged; still the stranger flashed again, closer now, its beam cutting a path across the waves like a road only he could walk. When the light finally touched the lantern room, his shadow stretched backward, tall and thin, toward the door behind him, and from the stairwell came the soft, wet sound of footsteps climbing.
