# lighthouse [continuation]
PROMPT: Continue this story in one vivid paragraph: 'The lighthouse keeper noticed the light was not his own.'

The lighthouse keeper noticed the light was not his own. It slid across the black water in a slow, wrong arc, green as bottle glass, and it did not come from his lantern, which still spun its faithful white blade through the fog. It came from the east, from a horizon where no chart marked a tower, and each time it swept the rocks it left a slick phosphorescent shine like something enormous turning over in its sleep. The keeper gripped the rail, counted the intervals—three short, one long, three short—and his breath caught: not a warning, but a question. Who keeps? His own beam stuttered once, twice, before he could think, and the sea answered with a groan of timber and iron. Out of the mist came a ship with sails furled and portholes glowing green, riding a swell too high for any wind. On its deck stood a figure in oilskins, faceless beneath a hat, lifting a lantern that held no flame, only the same impossible light. The keeper raised his hand, and the stranger raised his, and between them the dark water became a road.
