# lighthouse [continuation]

The lighthouse keeper noticed the light was not his own. It swept the sea in a slow, silver arc, colder than the amber breath of his old wick, and it came from the lantern room above him where no one else had climbed. He gripped the rail, knuckles whitening, as the beam paused on a black reef that hadn’t been there at dusk, then snapped toward the harbor like a searching eye. Beneath it, the water began to answer—faint green sparks rising in columns, gulls wheeling in silent circles, and the bell buoy tolling a note that belonged to no tide. When he finally climbed the stairs, breath ragged, the door stood open and the great lens turned by itself, polished to a mirror, reflecting not his face but a stranger in oilskins standing behind him, holding a lantern that burned with the exact color of his own.
