# market_dawn [prose]
PROMPT: Describe a street market at dawn in vivid sensory detail, at least 150 words.

Dawn arrives not as a sudden brightness but as a slow bruise of violet and rose over the rooftops. The street market is already awake, though half-dressed: metal shutters rattle up, wooden crates thud onto wet cobblestones, and canvas awnings snap like sails. The air is cool and damp, smelling of rain, diesel, and the first bitter coffee steaming from a vendor’s dented urn. Under yellow bulbs, pyramids of oranges glow; tomatoes shine like lacquered jewels; bundles of mint and cilantro release a green sharpness when brushed. A fishmonger hoses down his stall, and the briny, metallic scent mixes with frying dough and cinnamon. Sounds layer: cart wheels squeaking, a radio murmuring old songs, vendors calling prices in hoarse, practiced voices, pigeons clattering for crumbs. Touch: cold mist on cheeks, warmth from a grill, rough burlap, slick fish scales. Taste: a torn piece of warm bread, sweet and yeasty, salt on lips. Everything feels provisional and urgent, a city stretching before full sun.
