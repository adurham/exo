# pencil_history [prose]
PROMPT: Write about the history of the pencil, at least 200 words.

The pencil’s history begins with ancient styl uses. Romans used lead rods, called plumbum, to mark papyrus, which is why we still say “lead pencil” even though pencils contain graphite, not lead. The word “pencil” comes from the Latin penicillus, meaning a small brush. True graphite pencils appeared in the 1560 s, after a huge pure graphite deposit was discovered in Borrowdale, England. Legend says a storm toppled a tree and exposed the black mineral. Shepherds reportedly used it to mark sheep, and the deposit became so valuable that armed guards protected the mines. Because Borrowdale graphite was soft, users wrapped chunks in string, leather, and sheepskin. In the 17th century German craftsmen in Nuremburg began gluing graphite into wooden cases, creating the familiar wooden pencils.

In 1795 the modern pencil was born. During the Napoleonic Wars, France was cut off from British graphite. French chemist Nicolas-Jacques Conté mixed powdered graphite and clay, shaped the mixture, and fired it in a kin. By changing the clay-to-graphite ratio, he could control hardness and darkness. This method is still used today. In 1812 the American cabinetmaker William Munro made the first U.S. wooden pencils. Henry David Thoreaux’s family ran a pencil factory, and Thoreaux improved graphite grinding. In the 1820s Sampson Mordan and others patented early mechanical pencils. In 185 8 Hymen Lipman patened the eraser-tipped pencil. By the late 19th century pencils were mass-produced and graded from hard to soft. In the 1890s an Austrian company painted its pencils yellow, a color that became iconic. Today billions of pencils are made each year, remaining simple, reliable tools.
