# tests_opinion [opinion]
PROMPT: Are standardized tests a good measure of intelligence? Write two paragraphs arguing a position.

Standardized tests are not a good measure of intelligence because they capture only a narrow slice of cognitive ability. They reward speed, memorization, test-taking strategy, and familiarity with particular question formats rather than the full range of thinking. A student can score high through tutoring and practice without being unusually creative, adaptable, or skilled at real-world problem-solving. Conversely, a highly intelligent person may freeze under time pressure, misread cultural references, or struggle with the test’s language demands. Intelligence is multidimensional—including spatial, interpersonal, emotional, and practical reasoning—while most standardized tests reduce it to verbal and quantitative performance.

Moreover, standardized test scores are heavily shaped by environment rather than innate intellect. Access to test prep, good schools, books, stable homes, and English-language exposure can raise scores, while poverty, bias, and anxiety can lower them. That means the tests often measure privilege and preparation as much as raw ability. They can be useful for comparing academic readiness in a limited way, but they are not a good standalone measure of intelligence. To assess intelligence well, we need multiple tools: projects, observations, performance tasks, and tests that account for culture and context.
